## Lesson 1.1 — Read the workstation before beginning

Objective: identify contamination routes and set up a clean work zone.

A client sees a finished nail service. A professional also sees the movement of hands, tools, products and waste. A clean-looking implement may still have been used. Keep used implements separate from cleaned and properly processed implements. Store ready-to-use equipment so it does not touch used items.

Before service, inspect the station, lighting, ventilation and access to hand hygiene. Check that reusable tools have completed the required processing. Identify single-use items and arrange disposal. Keep product containers closed when not in use. Follow the workplace's approved cleaning and exposure-control procedures.

A glove does not make a contaminated surface clean. If you handle a used item and then touch ready-to-use equipment, you can transfer contamination. Pause, correct the contamination and perform the appropriate hand hygiene and glove change before continuing.

Worked example: A file from the previous service is beside the new client's towel. Do not assume it is reusable or processed. Remove it from the ready-to-use zone and follow the applicable single-use or reprocessing procedure.

Workbook: draw three labeled zones—ready-to-use, active service and used/waste. Trace how a tool moves without returning a used tool to clean storage.

Recap: Separate ready-to-use items from used items. A tidy appearance does not prove processing. Correct contamination before service.

## Lesson 1.2 — Cleaning, disinfection and the label

Objective: explain why cleaning and disinfection are different steps and locate the required directions.

Cleaning removes soil. Disinfection uses an appropriate product or process to reduce targeted microorganisms on the labeled surfaces. Do not use these words interchangeably. Reusable tools require the processing appropriate to their material, intended use and applicable rules.

Read the disinfectant label before use. Confirm intended use, dilution, protective measures and contact time. A label-specific time is not interchangeable with a time remembered from a different product. Keep the surface in the condition required by the label for the full contact period. Follow any required rinsing, drying and storage directions.

Do not improvise chemical mixtures. Product labels and safety data sheets answer different questions: the label provides use instructions; the SDS provides hazard and safety information. Ask the supervisor when the instructions are missing or unclear.

Worked example: A tool has visible residue. A quick spray is not evidence of complete processing. Remove the item from service and follow cleaning and appropriate disinfection instructions before returning it to storage.

Workbook: use the actual workplace product label to record product name, approved use, dilution if required, contact time, protective measures and storage directions. Have the supervisor check each answer.

Recap: Remove soil, use the correct process, follow the exact label and protect clean storage.

## Lesson 1.3 — Stop decisions and client communication

Objective: pause when service conditions are unsafe and document facts without diagnosing.

Ask the client about the requested service and relevant concerns using the approved intake process. Observe the service area without claiming to diagnose a disease. If there is broken skin, unexpected pain or another concern requiring assessment, pause and consult the qualified supervisor. Never cover a concern merely to finish the appointment.

Explain the decision calmly: “I need my supervisor to check whether we can safely perform this service today.” Do not promise that a cosmetic service will treat an infection. Follow the site's incident and exposure procedures if blood or another exposure occurs.

Document the client's report, your observation, the action taken and the supervisor involved. Keep records in the authorized system. Obtain required consent for images; do not use a personal social account as a record system.

Workbook: write a fictional service-paused note. Replace judgments such as “bad nails” with factual observations and client statements.

Recap: Safety can require a pause. Explain respectfully, escalate and document objectively.

## Knowledge check

Instructional passing gate: 80% (4/5). This is an institutional assessment criterion, not a state examination score.

1. A used tool is beside clean implements. Best action? A Proceed. B Separate it and follow required processing. C Wipe with a towel only. D Ask the client to decide.
2. Where is product contact time verified? A A social post. B An unrelated bottle. C The actual product label. D The service price list.
3. Which statement is correct? A Cleaning and disinfection mean the same thing. B Gloves replace hand hygiene. C All tools are reusable. D Cleaning removes soil; appropriate disinfection is a separate required process.
4. A client reports unexpected pain. Best action? A Pause and consult the qualified supervisor. B Cover the area. C Promise a cure. D Continue faster.
5. Which record is appropriate? A An unconfirmed diagnosis. B A factual report of the concern, pause and supervisor involvement. C A client photo on a personal account. D No note.

Instructor key: 1 B, 2 C, 3 D, 4 A, 5 B. Remediate incorrect answers using the specific scenario, then reassess.

## Supervised demonstration

Using a simulated station, identify clean/used zones; locate the product label and SDS; explain label-specific processing; identify single-use items; explain a pause decision; complete a factual record.

Each step is scored 0 (not demonstrated), 1 (coached), or 2 (independent in this task). Require 2 on each step before passing this classroom demonstration. A missed contamination or stop decision requires remediation. Link instruction to manicurist-A; do not automatically mark the occupational competency complete.
