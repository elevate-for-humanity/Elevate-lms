## Build each teaching moment

1. Start with the existing lesson's authorized source, objectives, quiz and practical criteria. Preserve its course/lesson IDs, enrollment/progress records and Appendix A requirements.
2. Break the method into focused actions. Show the step number first, then its heading, then concise instructions beside the complete photograph. Each teaching moment should explain what to do, demonstrate it, and give a fresh recap or practice question.
3. Assign an image or licensed Envato clip to every action. Confirm the visible action matches the narration. Generate supporting photos when needed, but use reviewed real footage for fluid physical techniques. Do not claim an AI photo proves live performance or licensing.
4. Show all tools needed for the service. For guarded cutting, show each relevant guard number and its manufacturer's nominal length; explain its intended use. Do not assume all manufacturers or lever positions give the same length. Render numbers and measurements as authored text, not AI-generated lettering.
5. For this service, include consultation, hair/scalp inspection, hand hygiene, tools, neck strip and cape, guard inspection/attachment, controlled cutting, transitions/blending, inspection of the result, finishing, tool/workstation cleanup and preparation for the next client. Follow product/equipment instructions; do not invent contact times or dilution ratios.
6. Write one complete narration track per action. Give each recap a new brief summary or question. **Do not replay earlier speech, loop a voice passage, overlap two narrators, or leave an unexplained silent teaching section.** Mute speech in demonstration footage while the instructor talks.
7. Generate and inspect every narration segment before assembling the film. Reject empty, truncated or inaudible output. Check unexpected silence in the actual waveform and listen across scene boundaries. Normal short speech pauses are acceptable; clearly label deliberate learner practice pauses.
8. Align captions and visual cues to the final recorded speech. Do not divide a long narration into evenly timed picture blocks. Remove padded seconds and silent opening/closing cards. At 30 fps, round a segment up only to its next frame, not to its next full second plus extra time.
9. Use restrained 0.75–1-second transitions. Keep visible instructional context during transitions. Fit pictures with `contain`, preserve their full authored boundaries, maintain natural contrast and keep text outside the picture. Do not leave a narrow picture strip beside a mostly empty slide.
10. Compare picture uses within each page. Reject redundant copies teaching the same point. A picture can be reused if its visible content/callouts demonstrate a genuinely different technique. Preserve the owner's original selected photograph at the opening of this reference lesson.
11. Attach the finished film to the existing lesson, and retain the interactive quiz, feedback, retry/remediation and practical checkoff below it. A bare MP4 does not contain these interactive controls. Provide a visible Play control and working captions.

## Picture generation directions

Use rich, sharp, realistic educational photographs with natural depth and lighting. Use the same coherent instructor/model context within a service. Keep the full tool, hand position and working area visible; leave ample framing so the photo can fit without cropping. Avoid navy/blue overlays, foggy contrast, collages baked into a single image, invented labels or irrelevant background clutter.

Example prompt structure: “Professional barber training photograph showing [one specific, source-supported action] with [required tools and guard]. Complete tool and working area in frame, clear hand placement, realistic hair texture, sharp natural contrast, warm neutral shop lighting, instructional close-up, no text, no watermark. Match the approved instructor/model reference.” Review anatomical/tool accuracy before accepting it. Build exact diagrams and guard labels separately with deterministic graphics.

## Timing and recreation notes

The original sample used forced word alignment against its recorded narration, separate numbered/text/image layers, contain-fit picture panels, timed crossfades and burned-in bottom captions. It was encoded as H.264/AAC MP4, 1280×720, 24 fps, approximately 9:48. A new production export can use 1920×1080, 30 fps while preserving the layout and cue order.

The baseline cue timestamps describe what the owner reviewed. **Do not copy the baseline's silent lead-ins or repeated recap audio.** Record fresh recap speech and rebuild the timing from the final audio. The new total duration is measured from that audio; it is not required to remain 9:48.

## Release checks

- Watch and listen to the entire encoded film. Confirm every picture/action is relevant, whole and sharp, with readable side instructions and matching bottom captions.
- Inspect every transition for repeated speech, overlapping tracks, silence, missing images or clipped text. Check the first and last frame as well as segment boundaries.
- Verify Play/Pause, mobile playback, captions, knowledge checks, feedback, retry, practical signoff, progress save and resume in the actual LMS.
- Confirm the lesson is attached to the correct existing course and visible through the enrolled student's dashboard. Do not create a replacement course shell or alter student records to simulate success.
- Publish only when the lesson's source, media, assessment and learner-flow checks pass. A URL, “rendering” flag or generated script alone is not completed media evidence.
